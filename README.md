# The :coffee: EXPRESSO Framework

> ### The Expresso Analogy
> Expresso is built around the analogy of an **espresso machine**.  
> 
> When making coffee, **you** are in **control** of the brew.
> 
> You can choose the **type of bean**, the **intensity** of the roast, and ultimately **how much** of the brewing process you want to **experience**.
> 
> `Expresso` applies the same intuition to software systems.
> 
> * **`Exposure Category`** is the **type of bean** — which domain of the system is allowed to express itself.
> * **`Exposure Level`** is the **intensity** — how deeply into that domain the system should expose its internal operations.
> * **`Event Index`** lets you focus on a particular **shot or moment** in the output stream.
> * **`ExposureTimeSource`** is the **brew clock** — providing the machine with an application's own sense of time, whether measured in simulation ticks, frames, cycles, or another custom clock.
> * **`The Bridge`** is the **machine's outlet** — allowing the resulting ~~**espresso**~~ **Exposure** to be extracted somewhere other than the ~~**original cup**~~ **native terminal**.
> 
> At the **lowest** setting, `Expresso` can provide only the essential results.
> 
> Increase the exposure, and progressively deeper layers of the system become visible — from major components, to intermediate operations, to internal logic, and finally to primitive operations.
> 
> Just as you do not need to taste every stage of the brewing process to enjoy the coffee, you do not need to expose every layer of a software system to use it.
>
> **Expresso does not ask everyone to become a barista.**
>
> It simply turns the machine into a **glass box**.
>
> **There is no pressure to understand everything.**
>
> The deeper layers are simply **available** whenever you want them.
> You can take the result and move on.
> You can look a little closer when something interests you.
> 
> > **The goal is not to expose everything, nor to enforce that everyone know everything.**
> >
> > The goal is to give anyone the **opportunity to build an intuition for the system they are using.**
> 
> For it is not about what someone **should know**.
> It is about what they **can know**.
>
